# 50 Psychology-Based Hook Templates

Use these when the idea is clear but the first line isn't strong enough to stop the scroll.
Pick a template. Replace the brackets. Keep it specific.

Templates keep their original numbers (#1–#50) and are grouped by the psychological trigger they rely on. "Visual" = suggested on-screen shot for video.

## Contents

1. Compressed Expertise — value shortcut
2. Contrarian / Myth-Busting — belief violation
3. Counterintuitive Method — "stop X, do Y"
4. Test & Experiment — open loop on an outcome
5. Loss Aversion / Mistakes — fear of doing it wrong
6. Contrast & Difference — side-by-side gap
7. Relatable Proof — social proof and borrowed success
8. Visual Reveal — pattern interrupt on an object or process
9. Aspiration & Progress — desired future, reassuring progress
10. Novelty & Future — change is coming
11. Hidden Lever — the small overlooked thing
12. Analogy & Borrowed Frameworks — familiar → complex
13. Direct Utility — practical, saveable

## 1. Compressed Expertise

**Why it works:** Promises years of someone else's effort in seconds. Signals credibility (time/money spent) and a high value-to-time ratio.

**#1** It took me [X years/months/reps] to learn [specific skill/lesson], but here's the [short version/core lesson] in [short timeframe].
- Example: "It took me 7 years to understand color grading, but here's the 30-second version for beginners."
- Visual: Old work vs current work side by side.

**#12** [Number] things I'd do first to go from [current state] to [desired state] in [timeframe].
- Example: "3 things I'd do first to go from zero clients to my first paid design project in 30 days."

**#13** I spent [money/time/reps] learning [topic]. Here are the [number] lessons I'd keep.
- Example: "I spent $4,000 learning photography. Here are the 5 lessons I'd keep."

**#14** I studied [number] [examples/creators/results] so you don't have to. These [number] patterns kept showing up.
- Example: "I studied 100 viral recipe videos so you don't have to. These 4 patterns kept showing up."
- Visual: A fast grid of the examples before the breakdown.

**#36** Here's the [number]-step version of [skill/process] I wish I had when I was [beginner state].
- Example: "Here's the 3-step version of meal prep I wish I had when I was working 10-hour shifts."

**#43** I looked at [number] failed [attempts/examples]. The same [mistake/pattern] kept appearing.
- Example: "I looked at 50 failed Etsy shops. The same product photo mistake kept appearing."

## 2. Contrarian / Myth-Busting

**Why it works:** Contradicts something the viewer believes. Cognitive dissonance creates an urge to resolve it — "wait, then what is it?"

**#2** I used to [common wrong action]. Now I [contrarian/new action]. Here's what changed.
- Example: "I used to post more to grow my page. Now I delete half my ideas before filming. Here's what changed."

**#3** Most people think [common belief] is why [problem happens]. The real reason is usually [overlooked cause].
- Example: "Most people think their skincare routine fails because of the products. The real reason is usually the order."

**#11** Your [problem] probably isn't from [obvious cause]. It's from [less obvious cause].
- Example: "Your bad sleep probably isn't from caffeine. It's from how late your brain gets light."

**#27** The problem with [popular advice] is that it ignores [real constraint].
- Example: "The problem with 'just post daily' is that it ignores how long good ideas take."

**#32** I thought [old belief] until [proof/event/example] showed me [new belief].
- Example: "I thought expensive cameras made better photos until this phone shot proved me wrong."

**#37** [Common advice] works only after [missing prerequisite].
- Example: "Posting daily works only after you know what your audience actually cares about."

**#41** Your [tool/skill/strategy] is not too weak. It's aimed at the wrong [audience/metric/problem].
- Example: "Your content isn't too weak. It's aimed at the wrong problem."

## 3. Counterintuitive Method

**Why it works:** Pairs a desired outcome with an unexpected route. The viewer wants the outcome and is curious why the "wrong-looking" path works.

**#4** To [desired outcome], stop doing [common method] and start doing [counterintuitive method] instead.
- Example: "To get better at Spanish, stop memorizing random words and start repeating full sentences instead."

**#21** The fastest way to [desired outcome] is not [common path]. It's [counterintuitive path].
- Example: "The fastest way to improve your writing is not writing more. It's rewriting one paragraph five times."

**#34** I'd rather have [unsexy asset] than [flashy asset] when trying to [desired outcome].
- Example: "I'd rather have 20 customer questions than a beautiful logo when trying to write a sales page."

**#48** Stop measuring [vanity metric]. Measure [better metric] instead.
- Example: "Stop measuring how many people liked the Reel. Measure how many profile visits it created."

## 4. Test & Experiment

**Why it works:** An unresolved result is an open loop. Viewers stay to find out the winner, and a real test adds credibility.

**#5** I tested [method A] against [method B] for [time/reps]. The winner was not the one I expected.
- Example: "I tested morning workouts against evening workouts for 30 days. The winner was not the one I expected."
- Visual: Simple A vs B split screen with the two methods.

**#9** Can you [achieve dream outcome] without [common constraint]? I tested [method] for [timeframe].
- Example: "Can you build muscle without eating six meals a day? I tested a simpler plan for 60 days."

**#22** I changed one thing in [process/system], and [specific result] changed.
- Example: "I changed one thing in my morning routine, and my afternoon energy changed."

**#39** I gave [same input/task] to [method A] and [method B]. Here's which one actually [desired action].
- Example: "I gave the same room to a cheap lamp and an expensive lamp. Here's which one made it look better."
- Visual: The same setup with only one variable changed.

## 5. Loss Aversion / Mistakes

**Why it works:** People are more motivated to avoid losses than to gain. "You might be doing this wrong" feels urgent and personal.

**#6** [Number] mistakes that make [target audience] [undesired outcome], even when they're doing [common good thing].
- Example: "5 mistakes that make beginner photographers take flat photos, even when they're using a good camera."

**#20** Before you [common action], check [overlooked variable].
- Example: "Before you buy another skincare product, check your cleanser first."

**#26** [Number] signs your [strategy/skill/system] is failing, even when [vanity metric] looks good.
- Example: "3 signs your Instagram page is failing, even when your Reels get views."

**#35** This [mistake] costs you [desired result] because it makes [specific mechanism] worse.
- Example: "This editing mistake costs you watch time because it makes the first sentence harder to follow."

**#38** The reason [target audience] stay stuck at [current state] is usually [specific bottleneck].
- Example: "The reason beginners stay stuck at basic piano songs is usually hand independence."

**#45** [Number] questions to ask before you [common action].
- Example: "5 questions to ask before you redesign your website."

## 6. Contrast & Difference

**Why it works:** Two things that look similar but produce different results — the brain wants to spot the difference.

**#7** This is the difference between [surface-level thing] and [thing that actually creates the result].
- Example: "This is the difference between writing a caption and writing a caption that gets people to DM you."
- Visual: Two examples on screen, point to the difference.

**#19** [Specific achievement] sounds impressive, but the useful part is [repeatable mechanism].
- Example: "A million-view Reel sounds impressive, but the useful part is the structure behind the first 3 seconds."

**#23** [Option A] vs [Option B]: one looks better, but the other gets [desired result].
- Example: "Pretty websites vs clear websites: one looks better, but the other gets more inquiries."
- Visual: Both versions side by side with one clear difference.

## 7. Relatable Proof

**Why it works:** Social proof from someone like the viewer ("if they could, I can"), or a result borrowed from a credible source.

**#8** [Relatable person] got [dream outcome] with [simple/low-resource condition]. Here's the method.
- Example: "This student learned conversational French with 20 minutes a day. Here's the method."

**#28** I copied [proven structure] from [niche/source]. Here's how I adapted it for [different niche].
- Example: "I copied a restaurant Reel structure and adapted it for a fitness coach."

**#30** Here's how [expert/creator/company] got [result] using [method] most people miss.
- Example: "Here's how a small bakery got repeat customers using one menu change most people miss."

**#46** The [audience] who get [result] usually do [specific action] before [obvious action].
- Example: "The creators who sell from content usually define the offer before they write the hook."

## 8. Visual Reveal

**Why it works:** A pattern interrupt — the viewer sees something ordinary and is told it means something bigger. Strongest when the visual carries the first second.

**#17** This [visual subject] looks like [obvious thing], but it actually explains [important lesson].
- Example: "This coffee order looks random, but it actually explains why most café menus don't sell."
- Visual: Start on the object, then reveal the business lesson.

**#18** Watch this [process/demo] for [short time]. It shows why [common belief] is wrong.
- Example: "Watch this editing timeline for 10 seconds. It shows why more cuts don't always mean better retention."
- Visual: The actual process happening, not just a talking head.

**#29** This [case/example] breaks the rule everyone repeats about [topic].
- Example: "This landing page breaks the rule everyone repeats about short copy."

**#40** This looks like [simple thing]. It's actually [deeper system].
- Example: "This looks like a grocery list. It's actually a system for eating healthy all week."
- Visual: Start with the simple object, then zoom out to the system.

## 9. Aspiration & Progress

**Why it works:** Lets the viewer see or feel the desired state, or reassures them that messy progress is normal — both are emotionally sticky.

**#10** Imagine [specific desired scene]. That starts with [vehicle/method].
- Example: "Imagine opening your laptop on Monday and already having the week's content planned. That starts with one content map."
- Visual: The desired scene first, then the tool/system behind it.

**#24** This is what [desired outcome] looks like before it looks impressive.
- Example: "This is what learning guitar looks like before it sounds good."
- Visual: The messy early stage, not only the polished result.

**#25** [Number] signs your [strategy/skill/system] is working, even before [obvious metric].
- Example: "4 signs your content strategy is working, even before your views go up."

## 10. Novelty & Future

**Why it works:** New information and predicted change trigger FOMO and the need to stay ahead.

**#15** [New tool/trend/change] is going to change how [audience] [does specific thing].
- Example: "AI search is going to change how local businesses get found online."

**#16** The future of [topic] doesn't look like [expected future]. It looks like [specific alternative].
- Example: "The future of fitness doesn't look like longer workouts. It looks like better recovery."

## 11. Hidden Lever

**Why it works:** "Small input, big output" feels achievable, and "nobody talks about it" implies insider knowledge.

**#33** Nobody talks about the [small/boring thing] that makes [desired outcome] easier.
- Example: "Nobody talks about the boring warm-up that makes public speaking easier."

**#47** This is the [small repeatable action] behind [big result].
- Example: "This is the 10-minute habit behind cleaner product photos."

## 12. Analogy & Borrowed Frameworks

**Why it works:** Maps something complex onto something already understood, or imports a trick from a field the viewer wouldn't expect.

**#31** [Known reference] is the easiest way to understand [complex topic].
- Example: "A restaurant menu is the easiest way to understand personal branding."
- Visual: The known reference first, then map it to the complex idea.

**#42** Steal this [structure/system] from [adjacent field] to [desired outcome].
- Example: "Steal this film director trick to make your educational videos easier to watch."

## 13. Direct Utility

**Why it works:** Clear, immediate usefulness. "Save this" and "what I'd fix first" promise something actionable and drive saves.

**#44** What I'd fix first in your [thing] to get [desired outcome] faster.
- Example: "What I'd fix first in your LinkedIn profile to get better clients faster."

**#49** [Number] ways to make [topic/process] easier without [common painful tradeoff].
- Example: "4 ways to make dieting easier without eating the same meal every day."

**#50** Save this before you [common/time-sensitive action]: [number] rules for [desired outcome].
- Example: "Save this before you film your next Reel: 5 rules for making the first 3 seconds clearer."

## Quick picker: idea type → triggers

| You have… | Try |
|---|---|
| Years of experience / money spent | Compressed Expertise (#1, #13, #36) |
| A belief your audience holds that's wrong | Contrarian (#3, #11, #27, #37) |
| A real A/B test or experiment | Test & Experiment (#5, #9, #39) |
| A common mistake to warn about | Loss Aversion (#6, #20, #35) |
| Two similar things with different results | Contrast (#7, #23) |
| A case study or someone else's result | Relatable Proof (#8, #30, #46) |
| A strong visual object or process | Visual Reveal (#17, #18, #40) |
| A before/after or messy-middle story | Aspiration & Progress (#10, #24) |
| A trend or new tool | Novelty & Future (#15, #16) |
| One small habit/tweak with outsized impact | Hidden Lever (#22, #33, #47) |
| A complex concept to simplify | Analogy (#31, #42) |
| A checklist, rules, or quick fixes | Direct Utility (#12, #45, #50) |
